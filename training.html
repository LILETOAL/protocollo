Training Protocol

Six sessions a week in a 65 minute working window, built around one ranking you chose yourself. Everything below is a consequence of that ranking, not a preference.

Priority 1PhysiqueDrives the rep ranges and the volume targets
Priority 2EnduranceBuys the Z2 finishers and the plyometrics
Priority 3Injury freeKills heavy singles and grinding sets
Priority 4Strength numbersSacrificed first, deliberately

Why that ranking is right, not a compromise

The concurrent training interference effect hits power hardest, maximal strength second, hypertrophy least. Putting physique first and strength last means the endurance work costs you almost nothing. Ranked the other way round, a cardio day would have to go.

The practical consequence: no heavy singles, no maxing, ever. Everything at 6 to 15 reps, RIR 1 to 3. Lower joint stress, lower CNS cost, which is what makes six sessions survivable against seven working days.

01 — THE WEEK

One lower day, three upper, two engines

Plyometrics sit on Monday as a primer: fresh CNS, they potentiate the squat that follows, they concentrate every bit of lower-body impact into one day leaving Monday to Saturday clear before the long run, and they train the broad jump benchmark directly.

Where the conditioning goes, and why

  • Z2 finishers on upper days only. Running after pressing and pulling costs nothing. Running after leg training competes directly for the recovery the quads need. That single placement buys 30 free aerobic minutes a week.
  • Sunday is rest and planning together. Pairing the lowest physical demand with the highest administrative load is the right way round. Saturday reviews the week that just ended.
  • Kettlebell swings suit you specifically. Hip hinge power with almost no eccentric loading, so they add nothing to the muscle damage Saturday's run already causes. Rare conditioning that does not compete with running.

The trade you made on purpose

One lower day is leg maintenance, not leg growth. It puts you at roughly 7 quad and 8 hamstring sets a week against a 10 to 20 productive range.

Legs are half a physique, and physique is priority one. Running adds some stimulus but mostly to endurance fibres, so it does not fill the gap.

Review in three months. If legs look behind, add the second lower day back rather than piling sets onto Monday. One session cannot productively absorb 18 or more hard leg sets.

What got fixed

  • Arms were at 5 and 5. Compound work gives biceps maybe half the stimulus of a direct set, so it does not fill the gap. Now 9 and 9.
  • Abs were at zero. Now 6, loaded and progressing, supersetted into rest periods so they cost no extra time.

What is still short, knowingly

  • Quads at 7 and hamstrings at 8. The one lower day. Revisit in three months.
  • Visible abs are a body fat outcome, not a volume one. The direct work builds the muscle so it is there when body fat drops. No amount of ab volume substitutes for the composition side.
04 — PROGRESSION

The block, and what RIR means

Four-week blocks from 7 September. The RIR ramp is the progression: the weight does not necessarily change across a block, the proximity to failure does.

WeekTarget RIRWhat that feels like
13Comfortable. Three reps left in the tank at the end of every set.
22Reps start slowing noticeably on the last two of a set.
31One rep visibly slower than the last. Hard, never grinding.
4 · deload4Easy on purpose. This is where the benchmarks land, fresh.

Reading RIR in the moment

Watch bar speed. Reps slowing noticeably is 2 to 3 RIR. One rep visibly slower than the last is 1. Having to grind is 0.

  • Percentages would not work here. They assume a known 1RM and a body that performs the same every day. Neither holds: you are not testing maxes, and a five-hour phase shift is running on top of seven working days. RIR self-corrects. On a bad morning RIR 2 just means less weight, and the stimulus is still right.
  • Most people underestimate. When it feels like 2 left there are often 4. At 5+ years you will be closer, but expect to be conservative early. Taking an isolation set to actual failure occasionally recalibrates the sense of it.
  • Above 15 reps RIR judgment is unreliable, which is why the high-rep isolation work is marked RIR 0 to 1. Take it close to failure and stop overthinking it.

Plyometrics: a different rule

  • Progress by intensity, not volume. Contacts stay near 50 all year and the exercises get harder. Adding reps to plyometrics turns a power session into bad conditioning.
  • Halve them through September, to about 25. They are the most CNS-dependent thing in the programme, and your CNS is exactly what the phase shift is disrupting.
  • Depth jumps only after eight consecutive weeks. Highest force exercise in the whole plan and the one with real injury potential if rushed.
  • They are here because they improve running economy, so they serve endurance, which is priority two, rather than power, which is priority four. That is what justifies the slot.

06 — WHAT TO EXPECT

September is a ramp, not a block

You will be weaker, and it is not a signal

Core temperature bottoms out 2 to 3 hours before habitual wake. During the shift that sits directly on top of the 06:00 training window: coldest tissue, lowest force output, worst coordination.

Expect three to four weeks of it. Strength tracks core temperature and adapts substantially once the rhythm moves with you. Warm up longer than you think you need to.

Real training starts 5 October.

The first challenge is attendance, nothing else

24 sessions in 4 weeks, twice. Not load, not times. You are building the schedule, and chasing numbers during a five-hour phase shift is how people conclude the schedule does not work.

The honest part: seven working days against six training days on a fixed 8 hours. Deep work you love spends the same recovery budget as deep work you do not. Enjoyment changes how it feels, not what it costs.

Landing days. 2 Sept gym at ~50% with technique work and no plyometrics · 3 Sept ~60%, still no plyometrics · 4 Sept the normal September ramp begins and plyometrics are allowed. Movement helps entrainment, so going easy beats total rest, but depth landings on a disrupted CNS with cold tissue is the one combination genuinely likely to hurt you.

Two things still open. Bodyweight has never been measured, which also blocks any load prescribed as a bodyweight multiple. And the kettlebell technique check is not booked: one session with someone competent, inside the first two weeks.
